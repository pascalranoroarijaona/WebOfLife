\begin{abstract}
Discrete global grid systems (DGGS) based on icosahedral hexagonal apertures, such as Uber H3, represent an increasingly dominant paradigm for multi-scale planetary simulation, overcoming the coordinate singularities and polar distortions inherent in latitude-longitude meshes. However, the formulation of conservative finite-volume transport (advective mass conservation and diffusive entropy generation) on spherical Riemannian manifolds ($\mathbb{S}^2$) requires exact geometric interface calculations. Non-coplanar cell neighbors, geodesic boundary segments, and pentagonal distortions prevent the assumption of idealized orthogonal regular hexagons. In this paper, we formalize the mathematical and computational design of \texttt{DetailedInterfaceNormalResult}, introduced in Sprint 067 of the \textit{Web of Life} engine. We provide explicit formulations for outward unit normal vectors constructed in the tangent plane of the interface midpoint, great-circle arc lengths, and centroid alignment cosine corrections. We prove that this formulation satisfies First Law flux antisymmetry and Second Law Clausius-Duhem non-negative entropy generation.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Formulating macroscopic transport equations (Navier-Stokes atmospheric advection, oceanic tracer dispersion, and terrestrial ecological diffusion) across a spherical planet of radius $R_{\oplus} = 6.3710088 \times 10^6\,\text{m}$ requires integrating flux fields over discrete cell boundaries:
\begin{equation}
  \frac{\partial}{\partial t} \int_{\Omega_i} \mathbf{S} \, d\Omega + \oint_{\partial \Omega_i} \mathbf{F} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}} \, d\Gamma = \int_{\Omega_i} \mathbf{Q} \, d\Omega
\end{equation}
where $\Omega_i \subset \mathbb{S}^2$ denotes the control volume of cell $i$, $\mathbf{S}$ is the conserved state tensor, $\mathbf{F}$ is the interface flux tensor, $\hat{\mathbf{n}}$ is the outward unit normal vector, and $\mathbf{Q}$ denotes local source-sink reactions.

While hexagonal discrete global grid systems provide quasi-uniform partitioning and isotropic spatial connectivity, the cells projected onto $\mathbb{S}^2$ are not planar. Boundary edges are great-circle geodesics, and cell centroids do not generally lie strictly perpendicular to boundary midpoints. Sprint 067 establishes the canonical contract \texttt{DetailedInterfaceNormalResult} in \texttt{src/spatial/h3\_types.ts} to supply the exact geometric coefficients required by monadic finite-volume operators.

\section{Spherical Coordinate Geometry and Boundary Metrics}

\subsection{Geodesic Interface Arc Length}
Let two adjacent cells $c_i, c_j \in \mathcal{H}_r$ at resolution $r$ share a common boundary arc defined by terminal vertices $\mathbf{v}_A, \mathbf{v}_B \in \mathbb{S}^2$, where $\|\mathbf{v}_A\| = \|\mathbf{v}_B\| = 1.0$.

\begin{definition}[Metric Boundary Arc Length]
The central angle $\Delta \sigma_{AB}$ subtended by the shared boundary edge is:
\begin{equation}
  \Delta \sigma_{AB} = 2 \arcsin\left(\frac{\|\mathbf{v}_B - \mathbf{v}_A\|}{2}\right)
\end{equation}
The boundary arc length $L_{ij}$ in physical meters is:
\begin{equation}
  L_{ij} = R_{\oplus} \Delta \sigma_{AB}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}
Because vertex connectivity is non-directional with respect to edge distance, $L_{ij} \equiv L_{ji}$.

\subsection{Tangent-Plane Boundary Unit Normal}
The geodesic midpoint of the boundary edge is:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf{m}_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf{v}_A + \mathbf{v}_B}{\|\mathbf{v}_A + \mathbf{v}_B\|} \in \mathbb{S}^2
\end{equation}
The unit tangent vector along the geodesic boundary is:
\begin{equation}
  \boldsymbol{\tau}_{AB} = \frac{\mathbf{v}_B - (\mathbf{v}_B \cdot \mathbf{v}_A)\mathbf{v}_A}{\|\mathbf{v}_B - (\mathbf{v}_B \cdot \mathbf{v}_A)\mathbf{v}_A\|}
\end{equation}
The outward unit normal vector $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij} \in T_{\mathbf{m}_{ij}}(\mathbb{S}^2)$ directed from cell $i$ to cell $j$ is computed via the spherical cross-product:
\begin{equation}
  \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij} = \boldsymbol{\tau}_{AB} \times \mathbf{m}_{ij}
\end{equation}
where the orientation satisfies $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij} \cdot (\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i) > 0$, for cell centroids $\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j \in \mathbb{S}^2$.

\subsection{Centroid Alignment Metric}
Let $\hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ij} = \frac{\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i}{\|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i\|}$ be the normalized chord connecting adjacent centroids. The directional alignment metric is:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_{ij} = \hat{\mathbf{d}}_{ij} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij}
\end{equation}
For regular planar hexagons, $\alpha_{ij} = 1.0$. On spherical discrete global grid systems, slight icosahedral skewness produces $\alpha_{ij} \in [0.82, 1.00]$. Incorporating $\alpha_{ij}$ ensures accurate projection of normal gradients from discrete finite differences.

\section{Thermodynamic Invariants}

\begin{theorem}[First Law Mass-Energy Conservation]
Let $u_{n, ij} = \mathbf{u}_{ij} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij}$ be the normal projected fluid velocity at the interface midpoint. The advective-diffusive flux $J_{ij}$ satisfies anti-symmetry:
\begin{equation}
  J_{ij} = -J_{ji}
\end{equation}
Consequently, net global mass change over a closed spherical manifold $\mathcal{M}$ satisfies $\sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \frac{d M_i}{dt} = 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Because $\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ji} = -\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij}$, the projected velocity satisfies $u_{n, ji} = \mathbf{u}_{ij} \cdot (-\hat{\mathbf{n}}_{ij}) = -u_{n, ij}$. The interface area $A_{ij} = L_{ij} H_{ij} = A_{ji}$. The upwind concentration selection $\rho_{ij}^*$ switches consistently, ensuring $\dot{\Phi}_{ij}^{\text{adv}} = -\dot{\Phi}_{ji}^{\text{adv}}$. Similarly, the centroid gradient $\frac{\Phi_j - \Phi_i}{\|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i\|} = -\frac{\Phi_i - \Phi_j}{\|\mathbf{x}_i - \mathbf{x}_j\|}$, and $\alpha_{ij} = \alpha_{ji}$. Summing over all internal shared edges yields complete pairwise cancellation:
\begin{equation}
  \sum_{i \in \mathcal{M}} \frac{d M_i}{dt} = -\sum_{(i,j)} (J_{ij} + J_{ji}) A_{ij} = 0
\end{equation}
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Second Law Clausius-Duhem Compliance]
Fourier thermal conduction scaled by alignment metric $\alpha_{ij}$ produces unconditionally non-negative entropy generation $\dot{S}_{\text{gen}, ij} \ge 0$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The conductive heat flux is $q_{ij}^{\text{cond}} = -\kappa \left(\frac{T_j - T_i}{\|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i\|}\right) \alpha_{ij}$. The rate of entropy generation across the interface is:
\begin{equation}
  \dot{S}_{\text{gen}, ij} = q_{ij}^{\text{cond}} A_{ij} \left(\frac{1}{T_j} - \frac{1}{T_i}\right) = \kappa A_{ij} \alpha_{ij} \frac{(T_i - T_j)^2}{\|\mathbf{x}_j - \mathbf{x}_i\| T_i T_j}
\end{equation}
Since thermal conductivity $\kappa > 0$, interface cross-section $A_{ij} > 0$, alignment metric $\alpha_{ij} > 0$, and absolute temperatures $T_i, T_j > 0$, the product is strictly non-negative for all states $(T_i, T_j)$.
\end{proof}

\section{TypeScript Implementation}

The canonical interface is declared in \texttt{src/spatial/h3\_types.ts}:
\begin{lstlisting}[language=bash]
export interface DetailedInterfaceNormalResult {
  readonly normal: readonly [number, number, number];
  readonly arcLengthMeters: number;
  readonly alignmentCos: number;
}
\end{lstlisting}

This zero-copy tuple interface avoids heap allocation per edge calculation and allows direct mapping into WebGL vertex attribute buffers and WebAssembly array pointers.

By incorporating outward unit normal vectors, geodesic arc lengths, and centroid alignment corrections, the \textit{Web of Life} engine ensures that planetary-scale advective and diffusive dynamics satisfy fundamental physical conservation laws.
