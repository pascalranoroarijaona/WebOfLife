\begin{abstract}
Complex ecosystem and biogeochemical simulations often risk silent thermodynamic degradation, where numerical round-offs, compounding errors, or improper flux calculations violate foundational laws of physics---namely, the conservation of matter (First Law) and non-negative entropy generation (Second Law). In Sprint 030, we establish a rigorous software and mathematical validation layer via \texttt{src/thermodynamics/state\_validator.ts}. This module acts as an immutable interceptor within the simulation's monadic transformation pipeline, enforcing strict predicate checks on thermodynamic state vectors prior to step execution. We formalize the mathematical boundaries of state vectors, outline the interface contracts, and present the TypeScript implementation that guarantees thermodynamic admissibility across all simulated EarthPod and biogeochemical cycles within the Web of Life architecture (\url{https://github.com/pascalranoroarijaona/WebOfLife}).
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction and Physical Principles}
The Web of Life simulation models biosphere dynamics by coupling matter circulation with energy dissipation. To maintain physical realism over extended temporal horizons, computational models must adhere to physical conservation laws:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First Law of Thermodynamics (Matter \& Energy Conservation):} Total mass and elemental stocks (Carbon, Nitrogen, Phosphorus, Water) within closed or openly coupled subsystems must balance strictly. Negative elemental stocks represent unphysical sinks or creation of matter out of nothing.
    \item \textbf{Second Law of Thermodynamics (Entropy Generation):} Internal entropy $S$ must remain non-negative ($S \ge 0$). Localized self-organization (biomass synthesis) must be accompanied by commensurate environmental exergy dissipation.
    \item \textbf{Absolute Temperature Bounds:} Kinetic temperature $T$ must satisfy $T \ge 0\,\text{K}$ in accordance with thermal limits.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Mathematical Formalization of State Validation}
Let a thermodynamic state vector $\Gamma$ be defined as:
\begin{equation}
    \Gamma = \{ E, S, T, \mathbf{M} \}
\end{equation}
Where:
\begin{itemize}
    \item $E \in \mathbb{R}$ is total system energy ($\text{Joules}$).
    \item $S \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is system entropy ($\text{J}\cdot\text{K}^{-1}$).
    \item $T \in \mathbb{R}_{\ge 0}$ is absolute temperature ($\text{Kelvin}$).
    \item $\mathbf{M} = \{ m_1, m_2, \dots, m_n \}$ represents elemental mass stocks ($\text{kg}$ or $\text{mol}$).
\end{itemize}

\noindent The validator enforces the following invariant predicates:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Existence Predicate:} $\forall p \in \{\text{energy}, \text{entropy}, \text{temperature}, \text{elementalStocks}\}, p \neq \text{undefined} \land p \neq \text{null}$.
    \item \textbf{Entropy Bound Predicate:} $S \ge 0$.
    \item \textbf{Temperature Bound Predicate:} $T \ge 0$.
    \item \textbf{Mass Conservation Predicate:} $\forall m_i \in \mathbf{M}, m_i \ge 0$.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Monad Integration and Implementation}
The validation wrapper integrates directly into the monadic execution pipeline (\texttt{ThermodynamicMonadProcess}):
\begin{equation}
    \text{Input State Vector} \longrightarrow \text{StateValidator.assertValid()} \xrightarrow{\text{Failure}} \text{Throw Thermodynamic Error}
\end{equation}
Upon successful validation, control is passed to the core transformation monad steps (Cycles / EarthPod updates).
