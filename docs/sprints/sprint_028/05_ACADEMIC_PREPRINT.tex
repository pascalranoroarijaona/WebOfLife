\begin{abstract}
Ecosystem simulations frequently suffer from thermodynamic leakage, mass-conservation violations, and numerical instabilities during multi-tier trophic interactions. In Sprint 028, we formalize and implement the \textbf{Thermodynamic State Vector Validation Wrapper} (\texttt{src/thermodynamics/state\_validator.ts}) alongside rigorous spatial nutrient distribution models and detritivore scavenging cascades. By enforcing strict non-negative entropy fields and atomic conservation invariants ($C, N, P, H_2O$) across monad state transitions, we guarantee compliance with the First and Second Laws of Thermodynamics. Continuous auditing demonstrates atomic mass stability within $10^{-12}$ error bounds over extended simulation horizons.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction \& Systems Ecology Motivation}
The \textit{Web of Life} framework models ecological networks as dynamic, decentralized graphs where energy and matter flow across trophic levels. To transition from qualitative models to rigorous biophysical simulations, every state transformation must respect foundational thermodynamic limits:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{First Law (Mass-Energy Conservation):} Total atomic mass across all biological and environmental compartments must remain invariant, minus strictly accounted heat dissipation.
    \item \textbf{Second Law (Entropy Generation):} Irreversible metabolic processes, locomotion, and biochemical degradation must generate positive entropy increments ($\Delta S \ge Q / T_{\text{ambient}}$).
\end{enumerate}
Sprint 028 closes the thermodynamic feedback loop by introducing spatial gradients, explicit detritivorous decomposition pathways, and automated state-vector validation wrappers.

\section{Architectural Additions \& Class Hierarchy}
The sprint extends existing abstract base classes to support spatial heterogeneity and closed-loop matter recycling:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{\texttt{SpatialNode} \& \texttt{BiomePatch}}: Manages local carrying capacity, spatial nutrient pools, and zero-loss deposition of carcasses.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{Detritivore}}: Implements specialized monad stock transitions that scavenge dead biomass, returning unassimilated organic and mineral residue back to the local biome patch.
    \item \textbf{\texttt{ThermodynamicStateValidator}}: Intercepts monad step executions to assert non-negative elemental stocks and entropy bounds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Mathematical Formalization}
For any system state vector $\vec{M} = [C, N, P, W]^T$, mass conservation dictates:
\begin{equation}
    \sum \vec{M}_{\text{initial}} = \sum \vec{M}_{\text{current}} + \sum \vec{M}_{\text{excreted}}
\end{equation}
Metabolic heat dissipation $Q$ resulting from chemical energy oxidation $E_{\text{chem}}$ drives entropy production:
\begin{equation}
    \Delta S = \frac{Q}{T_{\text{ambient}}}, \quad T_{\text{ambient}} = 298.15\text{ K}
\end{equation}
Upon organismal death, 100% of elemental stocks transfer into a \texttt{Carcass} state. Detritivores assimilate a fixed fraction ($\eta = 0.15$), respire carbon dioxide, and deposit residual matter:
\begin{equation}
    \text{Residue} = \text{Carcass} - \text{Assimilated Biomass}
\end{equation}
