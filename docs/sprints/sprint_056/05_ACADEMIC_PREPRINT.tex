\begin{abstract}
Discrete global grid systems (DGGS), particularly hierarchical hexagonal tessellations such as Uber H3 on the two-sphere $S^2$, provide a uniform geometric substrate for planetary biogeochemical simulations. However, discrete spatial advection and diffusion models are acutely vulnerable to floating-point coordinate drift, unprojected planar transformations, and pole singularity breaches. Unchecked, invalid coordinate representations trigger metric breakdown ($d_{ij} \le 0$ or $d_{ij} \in \mathbb{C}$), generating silent thermodynamic leaks that violate both the First Law of Thermodynamics (mass and energy conservation) and the Second Law (entropy production non-negativity). In this paper, we formalize the mathematical requirements for coordinate domain validation on spherical manifolds and present the design of the \texttt{assertValidCoordinatePair} invariant operator implemented in Sprint 056 of the open-source \textit{Web of Life} ecosystem engine. We prove that pre-metric boundary assertion guarantees strict positivity of the Riemannian metric distance, enforces conservation closures across advective-diffusive monads, and prevents computational $\text{NaN}$ contagion across planetary state tensors.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

In high-resolution computational earth system modeling and decentralized trophic web simulations, continuous spherical geometries must be discretized into finite computational cells~\cite{sahr2003geodesic}. The \textit{Web of Life} platform utilizes the Uber H3 discrete global grid system to partition planetary surface dynamics into hierarchical hexagonal cells~\cite{weboflife2025}. 

Each cell $c \in \mathcal{H}_3$ acts as a thermodynamic open system exchanging mass (carbon $M_C$, nitrogen $M_N$, phosphorus $M_P$, moisture $M_{\text{H}_2\text{O}}$, dissolved oxygen $M_{\text{O}_2}$) and internal thermal energy $U$ with contiguous neighbors. Transport rates depend fundamentally on the geodesic distance metric $d_{ij} = d(\mathbf{x}_i, \mathbf{x}_j)$ and cross-sectional interface boundaries $A_{ij}$. 

When coordinate pairs $(\phi, \lambda)$ describing cell centroids or particle positions escape the canonical spherical domain $\mathcal{D}_{\text{geo}} = [-\pi/2, \pi/2] \times [-\pi, \pi]$, standard geodesic calculations encounter mathematical singularities. An unbounded latitude $|\phi| > \pi/2$ causes metric evaluation failures in the haversine and spherical law of cosines equations, yielding negative radicands and inducing IEEE 754 floating-point $\text{NaN}$ values. The resulting network disconnection breaks the graph adjacency topology $\mathcal{G} = (\mathcal{V}, \mathcal{E})$, allowing conserved biogeochemical stocks to evacuate into unmapped memory addresses.

To prevent silent mass-energy dissipation and non-physical entropy destruction, we introduce the formal invariant boundary operator \texttt{assertValidCoordinatePair} within the spatial adjacency subsystem (\texttt{src/spatial/h3\_adjacency.ts}).

\section{Thermodynamic \& Geodesic Formulations}

\subsection{First Law: Conservation of Conserved Biogeochemical Stocks}

Let the state vector of cell $i$ be defined as:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{S}_i = \begin{bmatrix} M_{C, i} & M_{N, i} & M_{P, i} & M_{\text{H}_2\text{O}, i} & M_{\text{O}_2, i} & U_i \end{bmatrix}^T
\end{equation}
The global continuity equation requires that in the absence of external boundary flux, the sum of internal deltas vanishes identically:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{i \in \mathcal{V}} \frac{d \mathbf{S}_i}{dt} = \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}
For any pair of topologically adjacent cells $\langle i, j \rangle \in \mathcal{E}$, the flux tensor $\mathbf{J}_{ij}$ across interface $A_{ij}$ must exhibit strict anti-symmetry:
\begin{equation}
\mathbf{J}_{ij} = -\mathbf{J}_{ji} \quad \implies \quad \Delta \mathbf{S}_i + \Delta \mathbf{S}_j = \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}
If coordinate corruption prevents cell $j$ from being identified as the terminal node of edge $e = (i, j)$, flux $\mathbf{J}_{ij}$ is subtracted from cell $i$ but never credited to cell $j$, creating a non-physical sink:
\begin{equation}
\sum_{c \in \mathcal{H}_3} \Delta \mathbf{S}_c = -\mathbf{J}_{ij} \neq \mathbf{0}
\end{equation}

\subsection{Second Law: Non-Negative Entropy Production}

Irreversible inter-cell advective-diffusive heat transfer $\dot{Q}_{ij}$ generates entropy $\dot{S}_{\text{gen}}$ according to the Clausius-Duhem inequality:
\begin{equation}
\dot{S}_{\text{gen}} = \dot{Q}_{ij} \left( \frac{1}{T_j} - \frac{1}{T_i} \right) = \kappa_{\text{eff}} \frac{A_{ij}}{d_{ij}} \frac{(T_i - T_j)^2}{T_i T_j} \ge 0
\end{equation}
where $\kappa_{\text{eff}} > 0$ is the effective thermal conductivity and $T_i, T_j > 0$ are absolute temperatures. 

If an invalid coordinate pair causes the distance metric $d_{ij}$ to flip sign ($d_{ij} < 0$), the entropy generation rate inverts:
\begin{equation}
\dot{S}_{\text{gen}} < 0
\end{equation}
constituting a spontaneous violation of the Second Law of Thermodynamics (spontaneous generation of exergy from ambient equilibrium). Ensuring $d_{ij} > 0$ unconditionally requires asserting that coordinates strictly reside on $S^2$.

\section{The Geodesic Boundary Domain}

\subsection{Spherical Domain Constraints}

The continuous terrestrial surface is parameterized by latitude $\phi$ and longitude $\lambda$. The valid coordinate space $\mathcal{D}_{\text{geo}}$ on $S^2$ is bounded by:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{D}_{\text{geo}} = \left\{ (\phi, \lambda) \in \mathbb{R}^2 \;\middle|\; -\frac{\pi}{2} \le \phi \le \frac{\pi}{2} \;\land\; -\pi \le \lambda \le \pi \right\}
\end{equation}
In degree-based floating-point representations with numerical tolerance $\epsilon = 10^{-9}$, this corresponds to:
\begin{align}
\phi &\in [-90.0 - \epsilon, \; +90.0 + \epsilon] \\
\lambda &\in [-180.0 - \epsilon, \; +180.0 + \epsilon]
\end{align}
When normalized positive longitude mode is enabled for wrapping azimuth systems, the longitudinal domain expands to:
\begin{equation}
\lambda_{360} \in [-180.0 - \epsilon, \; +360.0 + \epsilon]
\end{equation}

\subsection{IEEE 754 Floating-Point Sanity}

To eliminate propagation of non-numeric states, coordinates must satisfy:
\begin{equation}
\text{isFinite}(\phi) \land \neg\text{isNaN}(\phi) \land \text{isFinite}(\lambda) \land \neg\text{isNaN}(\lambda)
\end{equation}
Subnormal inputs, positive infinity, negative infinity, and `NaN` are immediately trapped by the assertion guard.

\section{Implementation Architecture}

\subsection{Error Hierarchy and Invariant Assertion Signature}

The assertion helper is integrated into \texttt{src/spatial/h3\_adjacency.ts}. Violations trigger a specialized domain exception \texttt{CoordinateBoundaryError}:

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Java]
export class CoordinateBoundaryError extends RangeError {
  public readonly latitude: number;
  public readonly longitude: number;
  public readonly violationContext?: string;

  constructor(message: string, lat: number, lon: number, context?: string) {
    super(`[CoordinateBoundaryError] ${message} (lat: ${lat}, lon: ${lon})${context ? ` in ${context}` : ""}`);
    this.name = "CoordinateBoundaryError";
    this.latitude = lat;
    this.longitude = lon;
    this.violationContext = context;
    Object.setPrototypeOf(this, CoordinateBoundaryError.prototype);
  }
}
\end{lstlisting}

The function signature leverages TypeScript assertion type predicates (\texttt{asserts lat is number}):

\begin{lstlisting}[language=Java]
export function assertValidCoordinatePair(

  maybeOptions?: CoordinateValidationOptions | string
): asserts latOrCoords is number;

\section{Conclusion}

The formal assertion of geodesic coordinate boundaries in discrete global grid adjacencies resolves a subtle but critical failure mode in planetary biogeochemical simulation: silent thermodynamic dissipation caused by coordinate singularities. By enforcing rigorous domain boundaries and IEEE 754 compliance at the edge of the \texttt{H3AdjacencyService}, the \textit{Web of Life} framework guarantees metric positivity, preserves Second Law entropy monotonicity, and assures exact conservation of mass and energy across spatial monad transformations.
